2.1 Purpose
This Business Requirements Document (BRD) defines the scope, functional requirements, and customizations for the Incident Management module within the Jethur GRC platform for ADF (Agricultural Development Fund).
The Jethur platform is an existing, operational GRC system. This document confirms the standard (out-of-the-box) features in scope, documents agreed customizations requiring development effort, and identifies open items that need further input from ADF (Agricultural Development Fund) before configuration can proceed.
2.2 Scope
This BRD covers one module within the platform:
? Incident Management (RootCyber Add-on): Incident Register, Action Plan, Incident Response Plan Tests, and Report.
Out of scope: Vulnerability Management (CS-VM), Asset Management (CS-AM), and Risk Management (CS-RM), excluded from this engagement; detailed security operations centre work inside the security information and event management platform; specialist digital forensics and incident response tooling, whose outputs are documented against the incident record rather than produced in the platform; and any third-party integrations unless stated.
All customizations, including minor field renames, mandatory-status changes, field additions or removals, new workflows, and entirely new sub-modules, are consolidated in the Customizations section of this document. Module sections describe the standard, out-of-the-box system only. Cross-references between module sections and their corresponding customization sub-sections are provided throughout.
2.3 Implementation Approach
Implementation follows five phases: Initiation, Planning, Execution, Monitoring & Control, and Closure. By signing this document, both parties acknowledge acceptance of the requirements defined herein.
3. Scope Change Control
Scope change control governs any modifications to the agreed requirements after this BRD has been signed, ensuring all deviations are assessed, approved, and documented before execution.
3.1 Change Request Process
Any stakeholder identifying a potential scope change must submit a formal Change Request including: description of the proposed change, business justification, anticipated benefits, and risks of deferral. Change Requests are reviewed by the project leads from both ADF (Agricultural Development Fund) and Jethur. If approved, the BRD is updated and the version history incremented.
3.2 Impact Assessment
Each Change Request is evaluated for impact on timeline, budget, resources, and technical complexity. Jethur will provide a written impact assessment within five (5) business days. Work will not commence until both parties have signed off on the impact assessment.

6. Incident Management
The base system provides an Incident module containing the Incident Register, a cross-incident Action Plan rollup, an incident response plan test register, and a Report catalogue. The Incident Register logs an incident and walks it through a fixed sequence of lifecycle stages, each held on its own tab of the incident detail page. CS-IR uses this module for cyber incidents. It complements the security operations centre and the security information and event management platform rather than replacing them: detailed operations inside those tools, and specialist digital forensics work, stay outside the platform, while their outputs are documented against the incident here.
[Open Item] Shared-module data segregation: the Incident module is shared between the Cybersecurity Department and Business Continuity Management. Confirm how incident records and workflow are segregated between the two so the register scoping and ownership model can be configured.
[Open Item] Requirement numbering: the requirement sequence for this module skips from FR-IR-04 to FR-IR-06. Confirm whether a requirement was dropped or the numbering simply skips.
6.1 Incident Register
The Incident Register lists the reported incidents with their reference number, title, category, severity, reporter, dates, and status. An incident record captures the title, description, incident date and time, location, category and sub-category, severity level, reporter, and responsible user, together with optional supporting-document attachments. Opening an incident displays the Overview tab, which carries a visual incident timeline, followed by the lifecycle stage tabs listed below.
For agreed customizations to this sub-module, see Sections 7.1 through 7.6.
6.1.1 Standard Features
Each incident is assigned a unique reference number on creation. The incident detail page exposes a fixed sequence of stage tabs beyond the Overview, covering investigation, root cause analysis, communication, action plans, closure, and post-implementation review. The incident timeline on the Overview tab tracks each of those stages as pending until a record is added on the corresponding tab, so progress is visible without a separate status field being maintained by hand. Every action taken on an incident is captured in the activity log. Deleting an incident is blocked once any lifecycle child record exists against it, so an incident that has been worked cannot be removed from the register. Visibility of incident records is restricted to authorised roles.
[Open Item] Visibility model: role-based restriction of incident records is in place, but the sensitivity classification intended to drive finer-grained visibility is specified in Section 7.4. Confirm what visibility logic is active on ADF (Agricultural Development Fund)'s build today.

The Action Plan screen is a cross-incident rollup listing every action plan raised across all incidents in one table, showing Title, the source incident, Severity Level, Responsible User, Target Date, and Status. It lets the Cybersecurity Department track remediation work across the whole incident population rather than incident by incident. Action plans are raised from an incident's own Action Plan tab; the rollup is a view over them.
6.2.1 Standard Features
Each action plan traces back to the incident that raised it. Corrective actions are required alongside the root cause analysis before an incident is formally closed. Action plans carry their own responsible user, severity, target date, and status so remediation is tracked independently of the incident's own stage progression.

6.4 Report
The Report screen offers a custom PDF report builder, in which the user selects modules, filters, and layout and chooses whether to include charts, tables, and summaries, alongside a set of pre-built reports. The pre-built set includes an incident report in PDF covering incident types, severity, impacted areas, root causes, resolution status, trends, and response timelines, together with Excel exports of the register. Reports export to PDF and Excel. The catalogue does not carry the operational dashboard, the service-level reports, or the monthly executive report defined for ADF (Agricultural Development Fund); those are specified as customization in Section 7.6.
For agreed customizations to this sub-module, see Section 7.6.

7.1 Incident Lifecycle and Ownership (Section 6.1)
This section covers customizations to Incident Register (Section 6.1).
ADF (Agricultural Development Fund) requires a seven-state incident lifecycle of New, Triage, Investigating, Containment, Eradication, Recovery, and Closed, with an incident owner drawn from the Cybersecurity Department held separately from the business owner of the affected area. The deployed module tracks progress through a fixed sequence of stage tabs covering investigation, root cause analysis, communication, action plans, closure, and post-implementation review, driven by the incident timeline rather than by a seven-state status field, and records a single responsible user.

This section covers customizations to Incident Register (Section 6.1).
ADF (Agricultural Development Fund) requires evidence such as logs, screenshots, and reports to be attached to an incident, classified by type and source, protected against alteration after upload, and covered by an access audit trail. The deployed register accepts supporting-document attachments but applies no evidence classification, no post-upload protection, and no evidence-specific access log. ADF (Agricultural Development Fund) also requires a sensitivity classification on the incident itself, separate from the incident category, to drive visibility.
One constraint is settled and is not an open question: the platform can record who uploaded an evidence item, who downloaded it, and who is authorised to view it, but it cannot log a user opening an item in place without downloading it. Evidence access controls and the audit trail are to be built to that capability.
[Open Item] Evidence protection mechanism: choose between locking an evidence attachment read-only after upload and retaining protected copies, since the requirement allows either.
[Open Item] Evidence taxonomy: supply the evidence type and source values, and confirm whether the source values reuse the incident source list.
